# Folklore & Myth Inspiration — Soul Snatchers

Reference sheet for entity design. Child-taking and soul-collecting myths
exist in nearly every culture; each entry below maps a folklore figure to
the gameplay gimmick it suggests. These are starting points, not final
designs — entities should be *inspired by* these, renamed and reshaped to
fit our own mythology.

Design bar for any entity (from `GAME_DESIGN.md`): a motive of its own,
learnable tells, real counterplay, and testimony worth dying for.

---

## Candidates for main entities

### The Erlking (German — Goethe's *Erlkönig*)
A spirit of the woods that lures children with promises **only the victim
can hear**. In Goethe's poem the father hears nothing; the child dies.
**Gimmick:** whispers a private lure to one player — teammates only see
their ally drifting off-path, acting strangely. The *witnesses* must
intervene. Purpose-built for our witness system.

### The Pied Piper (German — Hamelin)
Led the town's children away with music, never seen again.
**Gimmick:** a melody that pulls players away from the group toward it.
Counterplay: break line-of-sound, plug ears (at the cost of hearing other
threats), or physically anchor a lured ally.

### Black Annis (English — Leicestershire)
A blue-faced hag with iron claws who snatched children and skinned them;
said to reach in through windows.
**Gimmick:** claws **through hedge walls** — the maze's walls themselves
become unsafe near her territory. Makes wall-hugging (default scared-player
behavior) risky. The most maze-native threat on this list.

### Jenny Greenteeth / Peg Powler (English)
Water hags that drag children into ponds and weirs, hidden under duckweed.
**Gimmick:** ambush predator in water rooms — fountains, lily ponds,
reflecting pools. Turns beautiful garden rooms into the most dangerous ones.

### The Sluagh (Irish / Scottish)
The host of the restless dead, flying in flocks at night, snatching souls.
Folklore says to keep west-facing windows shut when someone is dying.
**Gimmick:** a periodic overhead sweep — get under cover (trellis, gazebo,
grotto) or be taken. Our open-sky equivalent of a Pressure blackout event.

### The Ankou (Breton)
Death's servant who drives a creaking cart collecting souls. You hear the
**squeaking axle** long before you see him. Never hurries.
**Gimmick:** slow, inevitable patrol with a long audio tell. Not an ambush
— pure dread and route management. Teaches players to navigate by sound.

### Changelings / the Fae (Celtic)
Fairies steal children and leave an imitation in their place.
**Gimmick:** an entity that **imitates another player**. In a game whose
whole culture is community trust, a mimic is devastating — and testimony
about "how to spot one" becomes the most valuable knowledge in the game.

### Baba Yaga (Slavic)
The ambiguous witch: sometimes devours, sometimes helps — always at a
price.
**Gimmick:** a **bargainer**, not a chaser. Offers deals (safe passage, a
snatched friend back, a shortcut to the center) with hidden costs. The
sinister version of A Hat in Time's Snatcher archetype. Could be a fixed
encounter in a specific garden room rather than a roamer.

### The Sandman (German — E.T.A. Hoffmann's dark original)
Not the friendly dust-sprinkler: Hoffmann's Sandman throws sand in
children's eyes until they fall out, and collects the eyes.
**Gimmick:** vision attacks — creeping blindness, forcing reliance on
teammates' callouts (community again) or on sound.

### El Coco / the Sack Man (Spanish / Latin American, widespread)
A shapeless watcher — often on rooftops — that carries children off in a
sack.
**Gimmick:** a stalker with an observation meter: being *watched* too long
marks you. Counterplay is breaking sightlines, not running.

## World elements (not enemies)

### Will-o'-the-wisps (pan-European)
False lights that lure night travelers off the safe path into mires.
**Use:** false candles in the maze — light that promises safety and lies.
In a game where the candle is the goal, false flames are thematically
perfect navigation hazards.

### Psyche — the soul as a moth (Greek)
The soul depicted as a moth or butterfly leaving the body.
**Use:** ambient soul-moths drawn to candle flame — the game's thesis in a
single image. Could double as diegetic UI: moths gather where souls were
snatched (ties into the "echoes" suggestion in the design doc).

## Deeper cuts (bench for later)

- **La Llorona** (Mexican) — the weeping woman; a crying audio cue that
  could be a warning, a lure, or both.
- **Grýla** (Icelandic) — mountain hag who gathers misbehaving children in
  a sack; seasonal-event material.
- **Bonhomme Sept-Heures** (Québécois) — the "7 o'clock man" who takes
  children out after curfew; suggests time-based danger (the maze after
  "nightfall").
- **Lamia / Lilith** (Greek / Mesopotamian) — archetypal child-stealing
  night spirits; tone reference more than mechanics.
- **Soul cages** (Irish — the merrow) — a sea-being keeps drowned souls in
  cages; where do *our* snatched souls go? Lore hook for what snatchers do
  with the children they take, and a possible rescue mechanic someday
  (`IDEAS.md` material).
