# Main Recurring Enemy — Candidates

The "signature" entity slot: what SCP-173 is to Containment Breach and the
Angler family is to Pressure. Design notes on the role itself, then
candidates. Feeds `GAME_DESIGN.md` → Next Steps #1 (design the first entity
end-to-end).

## What the role actually is

The main recurring enemy is not the scariest thing in the game — it is the
**rhythm section**:

- **One simple rule**, learnable within a death or two (don't blink / hide
  at the flicker). Everything else in the game is paced around it.
- **High encounter frequency.** It defines the moment-to-moment feel of a
  run more than the maze layout does.
- **Skill expression that deepens forever** — the rule is binary for
  beginners and becomes a dance for veterans.
- **Variant potential** — remix the rule later (Angler → Froger/Blitz/…)
  without new tech.

**Multiplayer warning:** a straight 173 clone ("freezes while observed") is
weak in squads — someone can always watch it. Whatever fills this slot must
stay scary with 4 souls in the corridor.

## Candidates

### 1. The Gardener ★ (recommended for the vertical slice)

The maze is *its* garden. Souls are weeds. It is not hunting you — it is
tidying. (Original; tonal cousins: the Ankou's patience, Hoffmann-era
uncanny servants.)

- **Rule:** it perceives **movement only**. A reverse-173: it makes statues
  of things that move; you survive by *being* the statue.
- **Tell:** rhythmic shear-snips and dragging clippings beyond the hedges.
  When the snipping **stops**, it is listening/looking — freeze.
- **Counterplay:** freeze on the silence. Advanced: move *during* the
  snips, masked by its own noise — its rhythm becomes your movement window.
- **Squad-proof:** the rule binds each soul individually; more players =
  more chances someone twitches.
- **Candle hook (variant tier):** a frozen soul's *flame* still flickers.
  Later variants notice flame-motion, so the carrier must cup their light —
  the most important soul is the hardest to hide.
- **Testimony reveals:** the freeze rule, safe distance, snip-rhythm timing.
- **Variants:** the Night Gardener (hunts by sound instead — invert the
  rule); one that "replants" caught souls as wax-coated topiary (ties into
  wax pools / memorial imagery).
- **Feasibility:** patrol path + movement check. The cheapest concept here
  to greybox.

### 2. The Ankou's Cart (promoted from the folklore sheet)

Death's servant on his creaking cart, collecting souls. Never hurries,
never stops, never surprised — pure inevitability.

- **Rule:** slow permanent patrol; the **squeaking axle** is audible through
  hedges long before contact. You navigate *around* a sound.
- **Systemic role (the killer feature):** it **collects snuffed candles**
  left in the maze. Fallen allies' testimony is on a clock: "we go back for
  their candle before the cart reaches it" becomes a real mid-run decision.
  This welds the recurring enemy directly to the snuffed-candle system.
- **Counterplay:** route management by ear; it turns slowly; it does not
  deviate for you unless you're in the path.
- **Testimony reveals:** patrol logic, that it takes candles, axle-distance
  calibration.
- **Variants:** the axle falls silent in the deep maze; lanterns of taken
  souls on the cart that cast revealing light.

### 3. The Wick (false flame)

A will-o'-the-wisp grown into the apex predator. From a distance it is
**indistinguishable from another soul carrying a candle**. It hunts by
hope — players walk toward it on purpose.

- **Rule:** distant light ≠ safety. Every light must be *verified*.
- **Tell:** **soul-moths gather at real flames and avoid the false one**
  (moths are already in the folklore doc as diegetic UI). Flame color /
  steadiness slightly wrong up close.
- **Counterplay:** verify by moths before approaching; carriers can
  deliberately signal (rhythmic cupping?) to prove they're real.
- **Thematic fit:** strongest of the list — the game's core promise (light
  = home) turned into its core lie.
- **Slot fit:** slightly weaker as the moment-to-moment chaser; ideal
  second entity. Makes *every* distant light a decision forever.

### 4. The Sluagh Sweep (from the folklore sheet)

The host of the restless dead, sweeping overhead in flocks. Our equivalent
of Pressure's blackout event — a **metronome**, not a monster.

- **Rule:** periodic overhead sweep; be under cover (trellis, gazebo,
  grotto) when it passes or be taken.
- **Tell:** wingbeats, dimming sky, moths scattering.
- **Role:** forces squad-wide cover decisions on a rhythm; keeps the maze
  crawl from going flat between entity encounters. Pairs with any main
  enemy rather than competing with it.
- **Variants:** sweeps that linger over a spot; false calms; sweeps drawn
  to lit candles (carrier endangers the squad under thin cover).

### 5. The Mourners

Veiled child statues scattered through the garden. They move when
unobserved. (Yes, weeping-angel/173 lineage — the garden setting makes
statuary native rather than borrowed.)

- **Rule:** frozen while observed — but **teammates' gazes count**, so the
  counterplay is spacing and watch rotation. The witness theme made
  mechanical and literal.
- **Squad-proofing:** in squads the base rule is *too* safe — that's the
  point; the danger is corners, splits, and the last soul walking backward.
- **Variant that fixes the ceiling:** statues that only freeze for
  **candlelight**, not gaze — the carrier becomes the squad's shield, and
  solo runs past a Mourner field become legendary.
- **Testimony reveals:** which statues in a region are real (per-region
  knowledge with actual reread value).

---

## Recommendation

Vertical slice: **the Gardener** as the main recurring enemy (readable
binary rule, squad-proof, maze-native, cheapest to build). Second entity:
**the Ankou** (plugs straight into snuffed candles). Ambient event layer:
**the Sluagh Sweep**. The Wick and the Mourners go to the roster bench —
both strong, neither right for slot #1.
